Stakeholders, mapped by place

There are three places people sit: inside ETK, outside ETK, and affected by the work without ever signing in. Each person below has a job to do in the tool, and a limit on what they can see.

Inside ETK 7 roles

Project worker (country staff, consultants)

Needs: to log progress fast, often on a phone with weak signal. Sees: their own projects. Does: creates projects, sends the monthly check-in, raises risks, uploads evidence. How often: weekly to monthly. Channel: phone and web; can reply by email.

Line manager / country lead

Needs: to know what's slipping before the client does. Sees: their team and their country. Does: approves or returns updates, reassigns work, reviews risks, signs off country reports. How often: daily approvals, monthly review.

Global lead and leadership

Needs: one view of every project and the decisions due. Sees: everything, summarised. Does: reads the weekly report, decides on escalated risks, approves external sharing. How often: weekly.

Finance and compliance

Needs: spend that matches the books, and grant rules met. Sees: budgets and spend across all projects. Does: confirms "spent to date" each month and flags ineligible costs. How often: monthly.

Business development

Needs: proof points and case studies for new work. Sees: approved results and stories. Does: exports approved figures. How often: as needed.

Comms and marketing

Needs: stories and figures that are safe to publish. Sees: approved, anonymised highlights. Does: requests a story; never sees personal data.

IT and data protection lead

Needs: control over who sees what, and evidence of compliance. Sees: access settings and the audit log, not project content. Does: manages users, approves new external access, handles requests from people to see or delete their data.

Outside ETK 4 roles

Funders and grant-makers

Needs: progress against the agreed indicators, and spend by budget line. Sees: only their grants, as approved snapshots. Does: reads and downloads grant reports, asks questions. How often: quarterly, plus on demand.

Clients (businesses entering African markets)

Needs: confidence their market entry is on track. Sees: their engagement's milestones, next steps and documents. Does: raises questions and approves deliverables. How often: monthly.

In-country delivery partners

Needs: an easy way to report their part. Sees: only their own submissions. Does: fills in a short form for their indicators and uploads attendance lists with consent. How often: monthly.

Government and trade bodies

Needs: totals for impact and trade, for policy and promotion. Sees: combined figures only, never individual projects unless agreed. Does: receives a summary pack. How often: twice a year.

Affected, never signed in protected

Beneficiaries (trainees, SMEs, communities)

Why they matter: their names, phone numbers and photos often sit in attendance lists and evidence. What the tool does: reports count people rather than naming them, records consent, keeps evidence in a locked store with a deletion date, and lets them ask to see or remove their data.

Staff as data subjects

Why they matter: workload and performance can be read from the tool. What the tool does: workload views are for line managers only, and are not used for ratings without a separate HR process.

Data protection, built in from the start

ETK holds client, staff and beneficiary data across several countries, each with its own law. The safest approach is to design the tool so most of it never needs personal data at all, and to protect tightly the little that does.

Several of these limit sending personal data across borders. Keeping personal data out of reports, and storing evidence in one controlled place, makes this much simpler.

How the tool protects data

  • Collect less. Results are counts and percentages, not lists of names. Forms ask for numbers first.
  • A separate evidence store for anything personal (attendance lists, photos), with consent recorded and a deletion date.
  • Access by role and by country: a worker sees their projects, a manager their country, and outsiders their snapshot.
  • Work accounts with two-step verification. Access ends when IT switches off the account.
  • An audit log of every change, approval, export and external view. It can't be edited.
  • Approved snapshots only leave ETK, through expiring personal links. No email attachments full of raw data.
  • AI with limits: names and contact details are removed before AI reads an update. ETK's data is not used to train models. A person approves every AI suggestion.
  • Hosting and encryption: data stored in the UK or EU, encrypted in transit and at rest. Daily backups.
  • People's rights: the privacy centre finds, exports or deletes someone's data on request (a subject access or erasure request).

Before launch: data protection checklist

  • Complete a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) for the tool.
  • Record the lawful basis for each kind of data: contract, legitimate interest or consent.
  • Sign data processing agreements with hosting, email and AI providers.
  • Set retention periods, e.g. evidence 2 years after the grant closes unless the funder requires longer.
  • Name the data protection lead, and each country's contact.
  • Update privacy notices for staff, partners and beneficiaries.
  • Agree a breach response: who decides, and the deadlines to notify regulators.
  • Train country leads on what may be uploaded, in a 20-minute session.

How this differs from Power BI

Power BI shows data that already exists somewhere else. ETK Projects is where project data is captured, owned, approved, tracked and shared. It can still feed Power BI for anyone who wants to analyse further.

Where Power BI is stronger, and how they work together

  • Open-ended analysis across many data sources (finance, CRM, HR) and very large datasets.
  • Custom visuals and deep slicing for analysts.
  • Recommendation: run both. ETK Projects is where the data is created and governed. A "Connect to Power BI" export gives analysts clean, approved data, with no copying spreadsheets.

Built for everyone at ETK, not just analysts

  • No training needed. Every screen has one job and plain words.
  • The home page fits the person: the worker sees what they owe, the manager what needs approval, leadership the decisions due.
  • Works on a phone with a weak signal.
  • English and French, with room for more languages.
  • Accessible: built to the WCAG 2.2 AA standard, with keyboard use, readable contrast and screen-reader labels.
  • Data you can trust, because every number has an owner, an approver and a history.
